Design of Newpaper Reading (MMC-NPR-V2.5)

[column 1]
Here is what NPR needs, in the order I'd do it. None of it needs to start before the General Assembly. Decisions to take with you first The source of the page: a photo from the camera, an uploaded scan, or both. The languages to read, which decide the recognition models and the voices. What ends a column: a wide gap, a rule, the next headline, the page's edge. Commands while reading, such as "stop", "repeat", "next column". Who speaks: Thalia for the invitations, and the same voice or another for the reading. The steps Bring the OCR AIM into DI, done properly. Port it to the current AIF, and give it an L3 in the Instance style with the Basic Visual Object as input. Remove the read-a-server-file-by-name branch. Add SkiaSharp's native libraries for Linux. Put its models in Models, with

[column 2]
their settings and provenance, and copy the Recognised Text schema (MMC-RTX) from D:\AI. Write its L2 from the L3, for your approval. Test it on newspaper scans you provide, kept outside the repository like the photos. A column-selection AIM (new). It takes the Recognised Text (every line with its box) and the point clicked. It gives back the text of that column: the lines below the point that overlap it horizontally, down to the column's end. It joins words hyphenated across lines. It needs a Data Type for a point on an image. If MPAI-OSD has none, that is an addition for you to decide. The NPR Module (L3): OCR, then column selection, then Response and Scene Rendering speaking in the page's language, with MAT's voices per language. The page is recognised once; each click after that only

[column 3]
selects a column. The workflow carries the Recognised Text from turn to turn, as MPD carries its dialogue memory. The workflow: Acquire the page and show it. The user clicks the top of a column, and NPR reads it. Thalia invites them to point at another; repeat until they stop. The clients: a new acquisition of "a point on the image shown", in both the browser and the desktop client, and in the workflow language. This is the only genuinely new kind of interaction. The Service: provider, codecs for Recognised Text and the point, configuration, and the Linux package, which adds about 100 MB of models. Tests: a few pages with the true text of some columns typed by you, judged on column boundaries and word accuracy. The speech is checked by Whisper, as for HCI. The App: descriptor, icon, MAS-App collection, your check in both clients, and a Linux trial in WSL.
